Spot the scams

  • The same listing reposted with a new code on the title
  • A photo you’ve already seen on a different listing
  • “Text me” and other ways to move you off the site
  • A price far too low against what it sells for
  • Sellers who joined Facebook this year, or have few ratings on eBay
$

Know what it’s worth

  • How far below or above the typical price each listing is, in dollars, from real eBay sales
  • The sales behind each number, and how far to trust it
  • Select a product name on any website, right-click, and look it up

  • When the seller answers, whether to take it

For the things you actually buy.

Buying a couch, a stroller or a riding mower from a stranger? Check it before you message the seller.

  • Likely scams grayed out and sorted last
  • Broken and untested listings hidden with one switch
  • A fair offer, and a price to walk away at

Selling things on? Reseller mode adds the likely fault, how hard it is to fix, parts, and the price gap after eBay fees.

Optional: an AI second opinion.

Tirekick also has AI features. They stay off until you turn them on in settings and connect an AI service yourself, and nothing is sent to it until you press Scan or Analyze, or turn on automatic scanning. Skip it and everything above still works.

  • A score from 0 to 100 and a one-line verdict on every listing
  • One listing up close: red flags and a fair offer
  • For something broken: the likely fault, how hard it is to fix and what the parts cost
  • Pick any AI service you have a key for (Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google Gemini, Mistral, OpenAI, OpenRouter, xAI), or a free model on your own computer with Ollama or LM Studio. With a small model, a scan of 50 listings usually costs less than a cent.
